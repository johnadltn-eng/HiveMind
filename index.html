Content Selection — The Golden Wheel Method
Before anything gets posted, here's the actual filter for choosing what to make — not just what to talk about.
Take a wheel that already works, and make it a golden wheel — stronger, faster, better. Applied to content: find a clip or format that's already proven to work, then make a version that's measurably better along specific, identifiable dimensions. Not a repost. A remake that outperforms the original.
What makes a strong candidate
  • Small account, big outlier — a video doing far more than that account normally does is the real signal, not raw view count
  • The more recent, the better — something posted in the last week or two beats an older viral hit
  • Search by hashtag — the same tags the original used surface more candidates in the same lane
  • Flawed but proven beats polished but unproven — rough edges in the original don't disqualify it, that gap is the opportunity
What "better" actually means
  • 01 Punchier hook — built to pull comments and shares, not just views
  • 02 Sharper visuals — better color, cleaner framing, higher-res source footage
  • 03 Balanced audio — keep it if it's already working, just pull the volume down so it's not jarring
  • 04 Tighter cut — if the same beat lands faster, cut it down
  • 05 Captions that match the content's identity — animated, styled to fit
  • 06 Metadata that pulls its weight — file name, caption, pinned comment, description all feeding the algorithm the same way they feed a viewer
This isn't a one-off trick. Doing it consistently for 30-60-90 days is what makes it reliably outperform. Carousels are a separate lane — not covered by this method.

Finding It — Where the Winning Content Actually Lives
The Golden Wheel Method only works if you're scouting the right accounts and formats in the first place. The sourcing process is the same no matter what the niche is — fitness, a local preschool, or a horror feature. What changes is where you point it.
Same tactic, two completely different niches, same result: scouting by hashtag and format for small-account outliers works whether the subject is a fitness page or a local preschool's Instagram. The niche doesn't change the mechanism — it just changes where you're looking. For Killing Kin, that means pointing the same process at accounts running vertical series and BTS content — the exact lane already proven to outperform here.
One account worth checking directly: Blake Ridder. His content sits in the same lane — vertical series, BTS — and is a strong structural reference. The move is to study his format beat for beat, then execute a version that's measurably better using the same levers above — sharper hook, cleaner cut, better captions. Not a copy. A better version of the same beat.

The Whisper, Tease, Shout Method
The rollout isn't one push on launch day — it's a deliberate build in volume and specificity, so that by the time Killing Kin actually drops, the audience already feels like they're part of something happening, not being told about something that already happened.

Whisper — reactivate, no hard sell
Steady, low-key posting resumes on Instagram and YouTube — the BTS/candid content already proven to work. No Killing Kin push yet, just consistent presence. The goal here is momentum, not announcement.
Tease — Killing Kin enters the feed
Hook-forward posts, cast candids, behind-the-scenes from the shoot. The existing teaser and poster get a second life — re-surfaced, re-cut, re-promoted. Specificity rises: this is real, it's coming, here's a taste.
Shout — daily, cross-platform, countdown
Frequency increases across every platform. A fixed countdown begins — 48 hours out, 24 hours out, 12 hours out — each post raising the specificity. Every post keeps pointing to the same place: the consolidated bio link and captions all drive to YouTube, no new destination introduced this late.
Launch — the release sequence
Instagram first, YouTube mirrored with an optimized title and description, Facebook auto-posting, TikTok reposting the release content natively. Full attention window, comments answered in real time.
Sustain — hold the cadence, don't vanish
Posting settles to a steady, still-elevated rhythm. Comments keep getting answered while interest is highest. This is also where the data shows what actually landed — informing what comes next.

Two Fast Wins, Before Anything Else
One clear destination, everywhere — that's the actual highest-leverage move here. Not the website: the website stays a landing page for investors and legitimacy, not a traffic destination. Every call-to-action points to YouTube instead.
Fix and simplify the Instagram bio link. There are 3 links right now, and one — the Killing Kin teaser — is broken. Consolidate to one link, pointing straight to YouTube, for the launch window. Less is more: one clear place to go beats three options, even a tidy link-tree.
Swap the YouTube featured video, sharpen the thumbnail. Killing Kin should be the first thing a new visitor sees — not older content. The current thumbnail doesn't create a question; it should make someone wonder "what's happening here?" before they even click.
Capture — Confirmed Not This Round
This launch is traffic-only, by design — get viewership up first, build the pitch-ready proof, worry about capturing an audience once there's real volume to capture.
Once traffic grows past where it sits today, the same idea used for the Whisper → Tease → Shout build applies to a future capture flow too: don't make the signup the reward, make it the bridge — the next release is what actually closes the loop. Worth building for launch two or three, not this one.
