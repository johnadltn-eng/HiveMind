The Platforms, In Depth
What goes up, how it's paced, and the reasoning behind both — grounded in what these accounts are already showing, not general best practice.

Instagram

North Star
What
Reels carry the weight — hook-forward text overlays, BTS/personality moments (makeup chair, director candids, on-set banter). Stories fill the space between reels as connective tissue: quick, unpolished, frequent. Feed/carousel posts are occasional, reserved for produced pieces — poster art, cast photography. Sequencing tactic: front-load Stories with the most engaging content first, then sandwich the Killing Kin CTA story in right after an outlier hits — riding a hot story's momentum into the ask instead of posting the CTA cold. This isn't just a launch-week move — see the standing tactic below.
How
4–5 Reels per week at baseline — proven to flex up to 2–3 Reels/day during a launch push without falling off. Stories daily, spiking to 3–4/day in bursts (at least once every two days). One carousel/feed post per week.
Why
536 followers, but reels are already pulling 690–5,775 views — well beyond follower count, real algorithmic reach. Stories back this up directly: averaging ~2.5K views, trending up, with one outlier hitting 7K in about 6 hours. This is the account with proof the pattern works; it sets the pace for everywhere else.
Standing tactic — Stories → Killing Kin, ongoing. Every time a Story is clearly landing, that's a chance to redirect some of that traffic to Killing Kin on YouTube — not just through launch, this runs indefinitely as part of the standard rhythm. Two ways to execute it: put the YouTube link directly on the high-performing story itself, or post it clean and follow immediately with a second, simple story whose only job is the Killing Kin CTA. Either way, the ask rides a hot story's momentum instead of going out cold. Build this into how Stories get posted as a habit, not a launch-only move — it's the ongoing, lowest-effort way to keep funneling warm traffic toward Killing Kin long after launch week is over.

YouTube

Full Commit
What
Shorts mirror whatever's winning on Instagram, plus dedicated BTS cut for the format. Long-form carries bigger behind-the-scenes pieces now. All 6 episodes release together: a single playlist chaining all six back-to-back, set as the channel's featured/top section, so any visitor lands directly in the full watch-through, not one episode. Titles and hashtags stay consistent on every upload — "Vertical Series" in the title, #verticalseries #BTS #film every time.
How
3–4 Shorts per week, one long-form upload per week baseline (biweekly if bandwidth is tight). New rule, post-launch: pause regular Shorts posting for several days (up to a week) once Killing Kin is live, so the Shorts tab isn't diluted with unrelated content — resume normal cadence after. Revisit tags/title/thumbnail/description roughly every 4 weeks to check search ranking — SEO here is ongoing maintenance, not a one-time step at upload.
Why
254 subscribers, 85 videos — already the most active platform. The "can of sauce as a weapon" BTS short pulled 3.7K views against the official trailer short's 850 — the same BTS-over-polish pattern from Instagram, confirmed independently here. Live proof metadata alone matters: one reel stuck at ~7 views got a new thumbnail and a more character-forward title, then hit ~1,000 views in 9 days with zero new content shot.

Facebook

Auto-Post
What
A full mirror of Instagram. No content made specifically for this platform.
How
Auto-post via crosspost, spot-checked weekly for rendering issues — captions or aspect ratio occasionally break on crosspost.
Why
1.2K followers, but posting activity here is brand new (~1 month in) and reel views (35–295) are low relative to the page's size. Not enough signal yet to justify dedicated content — the audience size doesn't currently reward the extra effort.

TikTok

Cross-Post
What
Only content that's already proven itself on Instagram or YouTube. No original TikTok production.
How
Reactive, not scheduled — whenever something clears the "real winner" bar elsewhere, it goes up here within a day or two while it's still current.
Why
Smallest account (34 followers), but the top video still hit ~41x follower count in views. The algorithm clearly rewards good content here too — it's just not the platform worth spending original production time on yet. Also a hard platform floor, not a strategy choice: the bio link can't go live until the account crosses 1,000 followers.
Content Mix Principle
Lean BTS, not polish. Across every platform, deliberately imperfect and authentic beats produced and trailer-style — that's not a preference, it's what the numbers already show, independently, in three places.
Defining a real winner: a post doing roughly 100x the account's normal view count is the bar for "this actually broke out" — their own baseline, not an industry benchmark. Don't expect that breakout by launch specifically — real outliers tend to surface after real volume, not from any single week. That's what triggers "double down on what's proven," not launch week itself.
Consistency before intensity. Hold the cadence above steady before chasing polish or virality directly. Winners get found through volume first — then double down on whatever's proven.
Ownership & Bandwidth
Who's actually running each platform day to day.
Jonathan currently handles YouTube (leaning Shorts over long-form). Hannah handles TikTok and Facebook, and will start helping with YouTube going forward as it becomes the primary vertical-series landing ground.
One platform, one app. If bandwidth gets tight, the fix isn't spreading thinner across all four platforms evenly — it's deliberately pulling back on one (TikTok is the obvious candidate) for a couple of weeks to concentrate on Instagram and YouTube, the two platforms actually carrying Killing Kin's launch.

T-minus 48 Hours — "2 days" framing
Countdown post, hook-forward. Instagram primary, YouTube mirrored, Facebook auto-posts from Instagram as usual. TikTok stays dark here — there's nothing proven yet to repost, and an original TikTok countdown post breaks the platform's cross-post-only role. Copy needs a real clock behind it (an actual release date), not invented scarcity.
T-minus 24 Hours — raise the specificity
Same Instagram + YouTube pairing, Facebook mirroring. The escalation from T-48 is concrete detail — a clip, a cast callout, a specific reason to be there day-of — not just repeating "coming soon" louder. TikTok still dark, same logic as T-48.

No new information here, pure anticipation — the tightest post of the three, designed to keep the countdown building without repeating what's already been said.
Launch — the hour it goes live
The full release sequence, in order: Instagram primary release post/reel, hook-forward → YouTube mirrored with an optimized title and description, not a raw upload → Facebook auto-posts from Instagram → TikTok native repost of the release content. This is TikTok's first appearance anywhere in the countdown arc — it stayed dark through T-48/T-24/T-12 specifically so it could cross-post an actual proven asset now, consistent with its cross-post-only role everywhere else in this plan. All 6 episodes go up together — the YouTube step includes publishing the full back-to-back playlist and setting it as the channel's featured section, and regular Shorts posting pauses starting this hour (see YouTube, above).
Sustain — the week after, still elevated
Cadence doesn't drop straight to baseline the day after launch. Posting holds well above the standing baseline through the following week, comments get answered in real time while attention is at its peak, and this is also where the data shows what's actually landing, informing what gets doubled down on next. Regular Shorts posting stays paused through this window so the Shorts tab keeps surfacing Killing Kin first.
Steady State — back to the floor
Once the sustain week's data shows what actually landed, cadence settles back to the standing baseline at the top of this document — 4–5 Reels/week + daily Stories on Instagram, 3–4 Shorts + one long-form on YouTube, Facebook mirroring, TikTok reactive. Regular Shorts posting resumes here. The Stories → Killing Kin standing tactic (see Instagram, above) keeps running indefinitely from here on — it doesn't stop when launch week ends. During any lull between productions, cycle back through the proven back-catalog rather than posting nothing — short-form content reaches new eyeballs even on a repeat, since it doesn't build the same depth of relationship long-form does. Not a step down in effort — the return to "consistency before intensity," the same principle that got these accounts here in the first place.
